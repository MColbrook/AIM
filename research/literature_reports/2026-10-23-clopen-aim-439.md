## 439 — Interior bounds for stable elliptic states with sign-changing reaction laws

**Target:** [Exact statement at the reviewed revision](https://github.com/MColbrook/AIM/blob/8729eab98bafe409b66c5e39253bf56569a62327/problems/439-stable-semilinear-sign-changing-interior-bound.md). Catalogue badge at the snapshot: 🔵 OPEN.

**Primary source:** [Long-Han Huang, Yuxuan Zhou and Wenming Zou, Interior Regularity for Stable Solutions of Semilinear Elliptic Equations with Sign-Changing Nonlinearities, arXiv:2610.21641v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21641v1) (15 October 2026).

**Location:** Theorem 1.2, estimate (main-holder0).

**Reported answer:** Affirmative: a bound with a constant depending only on the dimension, for every stable solution and every reaction law of either sign.

**Full-target correspondence:** Theorem 1.2 gives $`[u]_{C^{\alpha_n}(B_{1/2})}\le C(n)\|Du\|_{L^2(B_{3/4})}`$ for every stable $`u\in C^2(B_1)`$ of $`-\Delta u=f(u)`$, with $`f`$ locally Lipschitz of any sign and $`2\le n\le 9`$. A function $`f\in C^1(\mathbb R)`$ is locally Lipschitz, and the paper's stability condition (stated with $`f'_-`$) reduces to the displayed one when $`f\in C^1`$. Averaging $`|u(x)|\le|u(z)|+|x-z|^{\alpha_n}[u]_{C^{\alpha_n}(B_{1/2})}`$ over $`z\in B_{1/2}`$ gives

```math
\|u\|_{L^\infty(B_{1/2})}\le C\bigl(\|u\|_{L^1(B_{1/2})}+\|Du\|_{L^2(B_{3/4})}\bigr)\le C_d\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(B_1)},
```

with $`C_d`$ depending only on $`d`$ and not on $`f`$. The paper does not state this $`L^\infty`$–$`W^{1,2}`$ form explicitly.

**Review:** On clopen.solutions the paper is recorded against the same problem ([OP-00571](https://clopen.solutions/OP-00571/)), with a full claim. Its automated review used two AI referees: Claude Fable 5.1 on the argument and Claude Opus 5.5 on whether the theorem matches the problem as stated. Held on one serious objection, which concerns the approximation results (Theorems 1.5–1.6) and not Theorem 1.2; the curator judged that it does not affect this target. Minor points: the paper calls its Hölder estimates sharp without showing the exponent is optimal, and it cites a radial energy estimate proved under a lower bound on $`f`$ without saying that the argument needs no sign condition.

**Evidence limits:** This is AI review, not human peer review, and the argument has not been independently audited here; no Lean verification is claimed. Under the [contribution policy](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#status-and-evidence) the entry is therefore Solution claimed, not Solved.

### Recovery lemma

Let `A` be the norm-closed unital real algebra generated by `T=J+S`, with `J^2=-I`, `||J||=1`, `JS=SJ`, and `||S||<=1/10`. All identities in the manuscript check:

`Q=I-JS`, `T=JQ`, `E=-T^2-I=-2JS-S^2`, and `Q^2=I+E`.

The bound `||E|| <= .21 < 1` permits the real-coefficient binomial series for `U=(I+E)^(1/2)` inside `A`. It has `U^2=Q^2` and

`||U-I|| <= 1-sqrt(.79) < .12`.

No unjustified uniqueness of arbitrary square roots is used. `U` and `Q` commute because `Q` commutes with `E`. Since `||U+Q-2I|| < .22`, `U+Q` is invertible in the ambient bounded-operator algebra. Factoring the difference of squares therefore proves `U=Q`. The Neumann series for `U^(-1)` lies in `A`, since `||U-I||<1`, and `TU^(-1)=J`. Thus **`J` really is a norm limit of real polynomials in `T`**, rather than merely a function requiring complex coefficients.

### Real irreducibility

A closed real `T`-invariant subspace is invariant under all real polynomials in `T`, then under their norm limits. Recovery of `J` makes it a complex subspace of the original complex space `Y`. It is invariant under `S=T-J`, so the choice of `S` forces it to be zero or all of `Y`. Scaling a complex invariant-subspace-free operator by a nonzero real scalar to make it small does not change its invariant subspaces. This proves the required real irreducibility without assuming that arbitrary real invariant subspaces of a complex operator are automatically complex.

Also `Q=I-JS` is invertible, so `T=JQ` is invertible. Dividing by its positive norm preserves every invariant subspace. The real interleaving isomorphism with `ell_1(R)` is bounded in both directions by the displayed elementary norm inequalities. If norm one is desired after transporting to the classical real `ell_1` norm, rescale once more after transport; invertibility and both lattices remain unchanged.

### Complexification and the entire lattice

The external scalar imaginary unit must be distinguished from `J`, and the manuscript does so. On the Taylor complexification,

`P_+=(I-iJ_C)/2`, `P_-=(I+iJ_C)/2`

are complementary bounded complex-linear projections commuting with `T_C`. Their nonzero ranges are `E_+={x-iJx}` and `E_-={x+iJx}`; equality of an external real and imaginary part immediately shows these vectors are nonzero when `x` is nonzero. Thus both are proper, closed and invariant.

The stronger four-element lattice assertion needs more than two commuting projections, but its proof supplies the necessary point. For the Taylor norm `||B_C||=||B||` for every bounded real `B`: the upper bound follows by taking the rotation supremum and the lower bound by restriction to real vectors. Therefore real-polynomial recovery of `J` persists in operator norm after complexification. Every closed complex `T_C`-invariant subspace is consequently `J_C`-invariant, hence invariant under each `P_+` and `P_-`. It splits into its intersections with their ranges. Possible graph subspaces between the two summands are excluded by this invariance, not ignored.

The map `F_+(x)=x-iJx` is complex linear for the original complex structure on `Y`, and is a topological isomorphism onto `E_+`. Indeed its real part recovers `x`, so its inverse is bounded. It intertwines the restriction of `T_C` with `S+iI`, which has the same complex invariant subspaces as `S`. The map `F_-(x)=x+iJx` is conjugate linear and likewise a topological isomorphism onto `E_-`. Pullback under a conjugate-linear isomorphism still sends a complex-linear subspace to a complex-linear subspace. The same argument with the conjugate operator makes this restriction irreducible as well. Both intersections can only be zero or their entire summand. This proves precisely

`Lat_C(T_C) = {0, E_+, E_-, X_C}`.

### Independent consequence of the invariant-subset theorem

The shorter argument is also valid. An operator on complex `ell_1` for which every nonzero vector has dense forward orbit has the same orbit closures on its underlying real space. Any nonzero closed real invariant subspace contains one of those dense orbits and must be the whole space. Its external complexification still commutes with `J_C` and preserves the two proper eigenspaces. This supplies the negative answer directly from the stronger established existence result. It does not automatically prove the four-element lattice for that unmodified operator, and the manuscript does not rely on it to do so.

The manuscript expressly avoids asserting novelty of this consequence. That qualification is appropriate: theorem-level validity and historical priority are different matters here.

### Exact 2023 conflict

Directly read [Gallardo-Gutierrez--Gonzalez-Dona, institutional full text](https://e-archivo.uc3m.es/rest/api/core/bitstreams/fb6949dd-5e47-4200-ae9e-51c65160aadd/content#page=30), Proposition 4.1, printed p. 184; Section 1.1, pp. 160--161; and the start of Section 4, p. 183. Proposition 4.1 takes a real Banach lattice with order induced by an unconditional basis and a bounded complex-linear operator on its complexification leaving the real part invariant. It equates (i) a nontrivial complex invariant subspace and (ii) a nontrivial real invariant subspace, also a moment condition. It does not state positivity or band-diagonality. The global convention restricts the spaces, while positivity is separately defined. The surrounding text adds no formal blanket positivity assumption for this proposition.

The example qualifies on the real Banach-lattice side. On the underlying real `ell_1(C)`, the interleaved coordinate basis is 1-unconditional and coordinatewise order is compatible with the norm `sum sqrt(a_n^2+b_n^2)`. More simply, transport the example to real `ell_1`, with its usual basis and order. Taylor and the usual lattice complexification norms are equivalent; closed subspaces and boundedness, and therefore the counterexample, do not depend on that choice.

In the numbering of the printed proposition the example violates **(i) implies (ii)**: the complexified operator has a proper invariant subspace, while its real restriction has none. The converse (ii) implies (i) follows by complexifying a real invariant subspace and remains valid. This is the direction stated in the submitted manuscript, and it is correct.

The proposition's introductory citation to Chalendar--Partington Chapter 9 is background, not a supplied proof of this equivalence; I have not audited that book. The present example is not a positive band-diagonal operator. It is therefore insufficient to reject the paper's separate results about such operators or to determine why the proposition is printed in this form. Keep the conflict statement at this precise level.

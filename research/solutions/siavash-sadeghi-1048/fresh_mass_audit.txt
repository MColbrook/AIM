1. INDEPENDENT RECONSTRUCTION AND ENERGY DERIVATION

Put k_m=m+theta and v_m=u_m/cosh(d k_m). Then
  V(s+ir)=sum_m v_m exp(i k_m s) exp(-k_m r),
  V(s-ir)=sum_m v_m exp(i k_m s) exp(+k_m r).
Their half-sum is sum_m v_m cosh(k_m r) exp(i k_m s).
This solves the scalar complex harmonic equation, has zero r derivative
at r=0, and equals u at r=d. No reality constraint on u or v is needed.

Orthogonality uses k_m-k_n=m-n, so it remains valid for every real phase.
For each nonzero k,
  integral_{-d}^d |k v exp(-k t)|^2 dt = k sinh(2dk)|v|^2.
Thus Q=(P/2)sum_m k_m sinh(2dk_m)|v_m|^2
      =P sum_m k_m tanh(dk_m)|u_m|^2,
with zero contribution from a zero-frequency constant. This matches (5)
and the stated single-strip energy, including its factor 1/2.

For fixed phase, exponential decay of 1/cosh(dk) makes V holomorphic in
the open doubled strip. On either edge its coefficient multiplier from
u is exp(+/-dk)/cosh(dk), whose modulus is at most 2. Edge convergence
therefore holds in H^{1/2}, not just in L^2. Also
  k sinh(2dk)/cosh^2(dk)=2 k tanh(dk),
so the derivative integral is finite when u is in H^{1/2}. The L^2
part of the H^1 norm is finite as well; the zero-frequency contribution
is 2dP|v_0|^2. Conversely, the H^1 Fourier weights of a holomorphic
Bloch function imply that v_m cosh(dk_m) are H^{1/2} coefficients.
Reconstruction is injective because cosh(dk_m)>0 on all real frequencies.

2. THE NON-EVEN, COMPLEX CROSS TERM

Use f(s)=sum_l f_l exp(ils). For finite sums the complex cross integral is
  I=P sum_{m,n} rho_{n-m} v_m conjugate(v_n) exp(d(n-m)).
Reality of rho gives conjugate(rho_{m-n})=rho_{n-m}; evenness is not used.
After conjugating I and swapping m,n,
  Re I=P sum_{m,n} rho_{n-m} cosh(d(n-m))
                         v_m conjugate(v_n)
      =P sum_{m,n} p_{n-m} v_m conjugate(v_n)
      =integral p(s)|V(s)|^2 ds.
The Bloch phase cancels before this calculation. One must not replace
rho_{n-m} by rho_{m-n} without conjugation; the proof does not do that.
Expanding |(V_++V_-)/2|^2 gives exactly (6), with the factors 1/4 and 1/2.

For general form-domain traces, Fourier truncations converge on both
edges and on the middle line in L^2. Since rho and p are bounded,
weighted products converge in L^1 by Cauchy-Schwarz. This justifies (7)
and (6) without additional smoothness or an illicit infinite rearrangement.
The harmonic middle-line interpretation of rho_k=p_k/cosh(dk) also works
for non-even p. Positive continuous boundary values on both edges give
min p <= rho <= max p by the maximum principle on the periodic cylinder.

3. PHYSICAL GEOMETRY, PERIOD, AND ORIENTATION

Under z=x+iy -> exp(-iz), the physical top is the unit circle and the
bottom is exp(-H(x))exp(-ix). The latter is analytic, simple and regular:
its argument fixes x modulo 2pi, and its derivative is its nonzero value
times (-H'(x)-i). Positive depth separates it from the top circle.
An annulus uniformization can be chosen to send outer to outer and inner
to inner. Its lift has deck transformation Z(w+2pi)=Z(w)+2pi. Equivalently,
the induced winding degree is +1, so there is no free horizontal scaling
left after setting the parameter period to 2pi. The annulus modulus is
retained as d, with inner standard-annulus radius exp(-d).

Analytic embedded boundary curves permit local analytic reflection for
the map and its inverse. On these compact boundary circles this gives
local analytic extension everywhere and nonvanishing boundary derivative.
This is the regularity fact needed here. I did not independently verify
the manuscript's bibliographic page numbers.

On the top, Z(s) is real, so Z'(s) is real and nonzero. On the bottom,
  Z(s-id)=X(s)+i[-h+b(X(s))],
  Z'(s-id)=X'(s)[1+i b'(X(s))].
If X' vanished, Z' would vanish. Thus X' has a constant nonzero sign.
Since X(s+P)-X(s)=P, its integral over one period is P, fixing the sign
as positive. The same reasoning fixes the positive sign on the top.
This does not confuse the induced boundary orientation (opposite on the
two boundary components) with the increasing horizontal parameter s.

Expand the periodic holomorphic derivative using its top coefficients:
  Z'(w)=sum_k rho_k exp(ikw),  rho_{-k}=conjugate(rho_k).
On the bottom this becomes sum_k rho_k exp(dk)exp(iks). Its real part
has coefficient
  [exp(dk)rho_k+exp(-dk)conjugate(rho_{-k})]/2
  =cosh(dk)rho_k.
Therefore p=Re Z'(s-id)=X'>0 is necessary for EVERY analytic graph in
the theorem. No symmetry of b, X, rho, or p has entered. Physical maps
also satisfy rho_0=p_0=1, consistently with the period; the abstract
weighted theorem may allow other means without harming this application.

The physical top line element is |Z'(s)|ds=rho(s)ds. The conformal
Dirichlet invariance holds componentwise for complex harmonic functions.
Zero Neumann data remain zero under the conformal normal scaling.
The period identity preserves exp(iPtheta) exactly. The energy and top
mass densities are periodic despite the functions being quasiperiodic,
so choosing curved instead of vertical sides for a fundamental cell
does not change either integral.

4. DIVIDED OUTPUT REGULARITY AND DENSITY

Finite Bloch Fourier sums are dense in H^{1/2} after removing the fixed
Bloch gauge. The strip form and bounded positive weighted mass are
continuous in that topology. Approximating a finite eigenfunction basis
therefore gives convergent finite Gram matrices and preserves independence
for sufficiently accurate approximation. No uniform approximation in n
or theta is required by the min-max comparison.

For the actual finite-series inputs, V is entire. V(a)=0 implies a zero
at every a+jP by its character. The denominator has only simple zeros
at this lattice, so V/B has removable singularities there. The denominator
is nonzero near either horizontal edge. Normal convergence of its product
there gives analytic extension, so W is smooth on the closed doubled
cell. Its reconstructed top trace is therefore admissible and smooth,
and belongs to the new character. The reconstruction remains injective
by the positive cosh multipliers. The proof never needs a bounded division
operator on the whole rough form domain. Positivity of p and equality of
edge moduli then legitimately imply M(W)>=M(V).

\section*{1. Exact target and covered class}
The repository is \url{https://github.com/MColbrook/AIM}. This package
addresses problem 1048 at revision
\begin{center}
\texttt{4f5b1659355cb61d194da94ef14615214e1363a8}.
\end{center}
The exact problem page and contribution instructions at that revision
are preserved in \texttt{TARGET\_SNAPSHOT.md} and
\texttt{CONTRIBUTING\_SNAPSHOT.md}. The repository status at that revision
is \emph{Partially resolved}. The proposed update preserves the original
problem statement and its ID.

Fix $h>0$ and a real, $2\pi$-periodic $b\in C^1(\mathbb R)$ with
$d_-:=\min_x(h-b(x))>0$. In
\[
\Omega_b=\{(x,y):-h+b(x)<y<0\},
\]
extend top traces harmonically, with homogeneous Neumann data on the
bottom, finite energy per period cell, and character $e^{2\pi i\theta}$.
The free-surface Dirichlet--Neumann operator is $f\mapsto\partial_y\Phi(x,0)$
in physical $L^2(dx)$. Order the eigenvalues separately in each fiber,
including multiplicities:
$\Lambda_0(\theta)\le\Lambda_1(\theta)\le\cdots$.

The proposed theorem is
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_n(\theta_a)\le\Lambda_{n+1}(\theta_b)
\quad(n\ge0,\ \theta_a,\theta_b\in\mathbb R).}
\]
Phase continuity and compactness of $[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$ give precisely
the repository target
\[
\max_\theta\Lambda_n(\theta)\le\min_\theta\Lambda_{n+1}(\theta).
\]
Conversely the max--min target implies each displayed pairwise inequality,
so these formulations have the same strength.

\textbf{There is no additional restriction on the profile.} The proof
claims the whole stated $C^1$ graph class, not just analytic or small
profiles. The bottom may be non-even and have nonzero mean; its amplitude
and slope have no imposed smallness bound. The given period need not be
fundamental. Every fixed profile has finite depth bounds and bounded slope
by periodicity and $C^1$ regularity, but no uniform bound across profiles
is assumed. Band touching and eigenvalue multiplicity are allowed.
The common form domain is $H^{1/2}$; its closed realization is the natural
extension of the harmonic problem stated for $H^1_{\rm loc}$ traces.

\section*{2. Map of the proof and hypothesis comparison}
The full argument is in \texttt{research\_nonoverlap\_proof.tex} and its PDF.
Its six sections have the following roles.

\textbf{Sections 1 and 5: the exact geometry supplies the mass identity.}
For an analytic graph, a conformal map $Z$ takes a strip of depth $d>0$
and horizontal period $P=2\pi$ to the physical channel, with
$Z(w+P)=Z(w)+P$. It preserves the Bloch character and Dirichlet energy.
The top measure is $dx=\rho(s)ds$ with $\rho(s)=Z'(s)>0$.
The bottom horizontal derivative is
\[
p(s)=\operatorname{Re}Z'(s-id)>0,\qquad p_k=\cosh(dk)\rho_k.
\]
Positivity of $p$ follows from the regular graph geometry. It is not
inferred from positivity of an arbitrary surface weight $\rho$.

For a holomorphic representation $V$ on the doubled strip, put
$V_\pm(s)=V(s\pm id)$ and $V_0(s)=V(s)$. The energy and physical mass are
\begin{align*}
Q(V)&=\frac12\int_{(0,P)\times(-d,d)}|V'|^2,\\
M(V)&=\frac14\int_0^P\rho(|V_+|^2+|V_-|^2)
       +\frac12\int_0^P p|V_0|^2.
\end{align*}
The cross term uses
$\operatorname{Re}\int\rho V_+\overline{V_-}=\int p|V_0|^2$.
Taking the real part is essential; the identity permits complex Bloch
data and non-even weights.

\textbf{Sections 2 and 3: one zero changes any nontrivial character.}
For $-d<y<d$, a normally convergent strip Blaschke product $B_y$ has one
simple zero per period, unit modulus on both horizontal edges, and
character parameter $\chi=(1-y/d)/2\in(0,1)$. Thus all nontrivial
characters occur. If $V$ vanishes at its chosen zero, $W=V/B_y$ is
holomorphic and represents a trace at phase $\theta-\chi$. On the
smooth functions used in min--max,
\[
M(W)\ge M(V),\qquad
Q(V)-Q(W)=\frac14\sum_{\pm}\int_0^P
   (\partial_\nu\log|B_y|)\,|W_\pm|^2\ge0.
\]
The quotient singularities are removable. Lateral boundary terms cancel
because both characters have unit modulus.

\textbf{Section 4: ordered min--max for arbitrary phase pairs.}
Approximate a source spectral space of complex dimension $n+2$ at phase
$\theta_b$ by finite Fourier traces. A single complex evaluation constraint
leaves dimension at least $n+1$. Choose $\chi=\theta_b-\theta_a$ modulo
one. Division and top reconstruction are injective and do not increase
the Rayleigh quotient. Min--max gives the claimed ordered comparison.
Coincident characters use ordinary eigenvalue ordering. This step assumes
neither simplicity nor tracking of analytic eigenvalue branches.

\textbf{Section 6: all $C^1$ graphs.}
Approximate $b$ in $C^1$ by real trigonometric polynomials, retaining a
positive lower depth. Pulling bulk energies back by $y=(h-b(x))z$ gives
uniformly convergent, uniformly elliptic coefficient matrices. Relative
form comparison yields convergence of every fixed ordered eigenvalue.
Pass the inequality for each fixed index and phase pair. A separate bulk
estimate proves phase continuity and hence existence of the band extrema.

\section*{3. Review provenance and questions for a reviewer}
The user requested an adversarial audit of the Blaschke factor, the mass
identity and the all-phase min--max argument. Three fresh AI review
contexts read the proof without the earlier audit reports. The coordinating
AI also checked the central identities and numerical diagnostics.

\begin{description}
\item[\texttt{fresh\_blaschke\_audit.txt}] Product convergence and nonvanishing,
exact zero set, boundary regularity, character sign, quotient removal and
energy comparison. Its stated scope does not independently cover the
geometric reduction and $C^1$ limit.
\item[\texttt{fresh\_mass\_audit.txt}] Complex reconstruction, the real-part
mass identity, conformal measure and positivity forced by graph geometry.
\item[\texttt{fresh\_minmax\_audit.txt}] Complex dimension, arbitrary phase
differences, ordered indices, multiplicities, finite Fourier approximation
and the $C^1$ passage.
\item[\texttt{research\_nonoverlap\_fresh\_audit.txt}] Coordinating audit and
the combined findings, including stress-test limitations.
\end{description}
These reports found no fatal flaw within their stated scopes. Their
terminology ``independent reviewer'' refers to separate AI contexts in
the same workflow. It does not mean independent human peer review.
The reports prompted expository clarifications now present in the proof;
they are not cryptographic attestations of the final file revision.
No human review has been reported. The package is a proposed mathematical
argument for examination, not a certification of correctness by the
repository or by a formal proof assistant.

The main points for fresh scrutiny are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does the normalized product converge to a nonzero holomorphic
function away from exactly the prescribed zeros, with the stated
character and sufficient regularity through both edges?
\item Does the symmetrized cross term equal the middle-line mass for all
complex Bloch data, and is $p>0$ supplied by every admissible analytic graph?
\item Does one complex constraint, followed by injective reconstruction,
give the correct $n+2$ to $n+1$ dimension comparison for every phase pair?
\item Does physical-domain form convergence remove the analytic-profile
restriction without adding a uniform slope, amplitude or spectral-index
assumption?
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Numerical evidence is supplementary.} The included NumPy script
checks 21 cases with complex coefficients, non-even positive weights,
several strip depths and phases near the character endpoints. It checks
product, phase, mass and energy identities and records reconstruction
errors. A deep-strip case has a large top-to-bottom norm ratio and visible
relative cancellation at the smaller bottom trace; the report retains
that limitation. The tests are ordinary floating-point diagnostics,
not interval certificates, exhaustive counterexample searches or proof
dependencies. A successful run cannot establish the theorem.

\textbf{Scope boundaries.} This claim concerns finite-depth, flat-top,
two-dimensional graph channels with homogeneous Neumann bottom data.
It does not assert the theorem for arbitrary positive strip weights,
nongraph channels, obstacles, pinched depths, different boundary conditions
or a shallow-water approximation. It also does not claim endpoint
localization of all band edges, monotonicity of band functions, simplicity
or strict opening of every gap. None of those stronger statements is
part of problem 1048's target.

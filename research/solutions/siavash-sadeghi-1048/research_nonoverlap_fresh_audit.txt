BLASCHKE FACTOR: ATTACKS AND FINDINGS

1. A bounded sequence of finite products need not have a nonzero limit.
   The proof needs more. Here each normalized tail factor is
   1+O(exp(-2*c*j*P)), locally uniformly, where c=pi/(4d)>0. The errors
   are summable. Beyond a finite index, analytic logarithms converge
   absolutely; their exponential is nonzero. Thus no unlisted zeros
   or identically zero limit are hidden in the infinite product.

2. The single factor's poles have heights 2d-y and -2d-y, modulo 4d.
   For any eta in (0,d-|y|), these poles are outside the closed enlarged
   strip |Im z|<=d+eta. Normal convergence there supplies the edge
   regularity and derivative convergence needed by Green's identity.

3. For the symmetric product B_N,
     B_N(z+P)/B_N(z)
       = b_y(z-s0+(N+1)P)/b_y(z-s0-NP).
   Its limit is b_+/b_-, not the inverse. Consequently
     B_y(z+P)=exp(i*pi*(1-y/d))*B_y(z),
     chi=(1-y/d)/2.
   Every nontrivial unitary character occurs. A one-zero factor would
   be obstructed if it were required to be periodic; that is not the
   assertion here. Its prescribed quasiperiodicity is essential.

4. The outward derivatives beta_+, beta_- are positive. An independent
   explicit check, with a0=pi/(2d), gives
     beta_+(s)=sum_j a0*cos(a0*y)/
       [cosh(a0*(s-s0-jP))-sin(a0*y)],
     beta_-(s)=sum_j a0*cos(a0*y)/
       [cosh(a0*(s-s0-jP))+sin(a0*y)].
   Their period integrals are pi*(1+y/d) and pi*(1-y/d), respectively.
   Their sum is 2pi; the independent normalization check is Q(B_y)=pi/2.

5. No singular interior boundary terms are omitted: V/B has removable
   singularities at every translated zero. Green's identity is applied
   to smooth V and W, not to the singular function log|B| in the bulk.
   Unit modulus of each Bloch multiplier makes the lateral terms cancel,
   separately for V and W even though their phases differ. Exactly
     Q(V)-Q(W)=(1/4) sum_edges integral beta |W|^2 >= 0.

6. As y approaches an edge, boundary energy can concentrate. There is
   no uniform edge regularity or uniform positive energy-loss bound in
   that limit. The proof fixes an interior y for each distinct phase
   pair and uses ordinary ordering for coincident characters. It does
   not take the invalid uniform endpoint limit. Flat-band touching is
   therefore consistent with the construction.

MASS IDENTITY: ATTACKS AND FINDINGS

For V(z)=sum_m v_m exp(i(m+theta)z), direct expansion gives
  I=integral rho V_+ conjugate(V_-)
   =P sum_(m,n) rho_(n-m) v_m conjugate(v_n) exp(d(n-m)).
Conjugation and index exchange, using only reality of rho, give
  Re I=P sum_(m,n) rho_(n-m) cosh(d(n-m)) v_m conjugate(v_n)
      =integral p|V_0|^2.
The phase cancels exactly. The unsymmetrized I need not be real.
Non-even weights and arbitrary complex coefficients are covered.

Thus for V=B W the edge masses are equal and
  M(W)-M(V)=(1/2) integral p(1-|B|^2)|W_0|^2 >= 0.
The actual hypothesis is p=cosh(dD)rho>0. Positive rho alone does not
justify this conclusion. For the analytic physical graph, the boundary
identity Z'(s-id)=X'(s)[1+i b'(X(s))] and nonvanishing conformal derivative
give X' nonzero. Degree +1 forces X'>0. Hence this needed hypothesis is
present for every analytic graph in the proof, without evenness or an
amplitude restriction. Physical dx equals rho ds, and the period and
Bloch character are preserved.

ALL-PHASE MIN-MAX: ATTACKS AND FINDINGS

Fix n and the input/output phases before taking any approximation.
Choose chi=(theta_b-theta_a) mod1 in (0,1), when the characters differ.
Finite Fourier approximation of the first n+2 eigenvectors gives a
complex (n+2)-dimensional space with maximal quotient at most
lambda_(n+1)(theta_b)+epsilon. Positivity of the approximated mass Gram
matrix preserves its dimension, including at multiple eigenvalues.

One complex evaluation condition V(s0+iy)=0 has complex codimension at
most one. All translated zeros then vanish automatically. Division and
top reconstruction are linear and injective; reconstruction has positive
multipliers cosh(d(m+theta_a)), including the zero frequency. The target
trial space has at least n+1 dimensions. Every vector has a no-larger
quotient. Therefore
  lambda_n(theta_a)<=lambda_(n+1)(theta_b)+epsilon.
Let epsilon decrease to zero. No uniformity in n, phase difference, or
division on the full rough form domain is needed. For coincident
characters, the original ordered inequality supplies the comparison.

GEOMETRY, REFERENCES, AND C1 LIMIT

The physical annulus has analytic disjoint regular boundary circles in
the analytic-bottom case. Ahlfors, Complex Analysis, third edition,
Chapter VI, Section 5.1, Theorem 10 (printed pp255-256), indeed supplies
annular uniformization when the connectivity is two. The local analytic
arc reflection argument in Sections 1.3-1.4 supplies the boundary
extension and nonvanishing derivative used in the proof. The root
reviewer rechecked the primary PDF during this audit:
https://www.matem.unam.mx/~hector/%5BLars_Ahlfors%5D_Complex_Analysis_%28Third_Edition%29.pdf
The argument applies the local reflection reasoning, not a simply
connected global theorem verbatim to an annulus.

The fixed-domain coefficient matrix in the C1 passage is correct.
Uniform ellipticity and C1 convergence yield pointwise relative matrix
inequalities on every complex covariant gradient, independently of
phase. Harmonic minimization preserves them. Min-max passes every fixed
ordered eigenvalue comparison to the C1 limit, including multiplicities.

For completeness, the proof now explicitly bounds the bulk L2 norm by
vertical energy and top mass, giving local phase-form estimates and
continuity of ordered eigenvalues. Compactness of the phase circle then
gives attained band extrema. This was an expository expansion, not a
repair to the pairwise inequality.

# 871. Are nontrivial planar lattice self-similar sets non-Minkowski-measurable?

**Area:** Fractal geometry
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`F\subset\mathbb R^2`$ be the attractor of finitely many contracting similarities $`S_i`$ satisfying the open set condition. Suppose its Minkowski dimension $`D`$ obeys $`1<D<2`$, and the additive subgroup generated by the logarithms of the contraction ratios of the $`S_i`$ is discrete (the *lattice* case). Must $`F`$ fail to be Minkowski measurable? Equivalently, must the normalized area of its $`\varepsilon`$-neighborhood fail to have a finite positive limit,

```math
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\varepsilon^{D-2}\,|\{x\in\mathbb R^2:\mathrm{dist}(x,F)\leq\varepsilon\}|\;?
```


The restriction $`1<D<2`$ follows the original high-dimensional formulation and excludes full-dimensional sets. Gatzouras proved the nonlattice implication. The converse is known in one dimension and for some planar families under additional geometric assumptions.

## Application

Minkowski content measures the leading area of thin neighborhoods of a fractal. A complete lattice/nonlattice criterion would tell when a self-similar planar geometry has a stable leading tube-volume coefficient, a quantity used in fractal geometry and related spectral asymptotics.

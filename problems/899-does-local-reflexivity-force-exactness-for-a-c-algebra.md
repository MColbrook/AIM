# 899. Does local reflexivity force exactness for a C*-algebra?

**Area:** Operator algebras and functional analysis
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`A`$ be a $`C^*`$-algebra that is **locally reflexive** in the usual operator-space sense: finite-dimensional pieces of its bidual $`A^{**}`$ can be approximated inside $`A`$ by completely contractive maps in the point weak-$`*`$ topology. **Must $`A`$ be exact?** Here exactness means that the minimal $`C^*`$-tensor product with $`A`$ preserves every short exact sequence of $`C^*`$-algebras. Equivalently, can an ordinarily locally reflexive but nonexact $`C^*`$-algebra exist?

The question ranges over all $`C^*`$-algebras; it does not impose separability, unitality, a group-algebra form or an approximation property. The corresponding implication for arbitrary operator spaces is false, so the $`C^*`$-algebra condition is essential.

## Application

Exactness governs whether tensoring by $`A`$ preserves quotient structure. An affirmative answer would turn the more local bidual-approximation property into a test for this global tensor-product behavior. A counterexample would separate two central regularity properties of $`C^*`$-algebras.

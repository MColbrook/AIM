# 921. Generic backward uniqueness for linear retarded equations

**Area:** Operators / delay differential equations
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Fix an integer $`n\ge1`$ and $`r>0`$, and set
```math
C_r=C([-r,0],\mathbb R^n),\qquad
\mathcal B_r=\mathcal L(C_r,\mathbb R^n),\qquad
\mathcal X=C^1(\mathbb R,\mathcal B_r).
```

Use the supremum norm on $`C_r`$, the bounded-operator norm on $`\mathcal B_r`$, and the compact $`C^1`$ topology on $`\mathcal X`$, generated by
```math
p_m(L)=\sup_{|t|\le m}\bigl(\|L(t)\|+\|L'(t)\|\bigr),\qquad m=1,2,\ldots.
```

For $`L\in\mathcal X`$, let $`T_L(t,\sigma)\phi=x_t`$ be the forward solution operator for
```math
x'(s)=L(s)x_s,\qquad x_\sigma=\phi,\qquad
x_s(\theta)=x(s+\theta)\quad(-r\le\theta\le0).
```

Is
```math
\mathcal G_{r,n}=
\{L\in\mathcal X:T_L(t,\sigma):C_r\to C_r
\text{ is injective for every }\sigma<t\}
```

residual in $`\mathcal X`$, for every $`n\ge1`$ and $`r>0`$?
Here residual means containing a countable intersection of open dense sets.

## Application

The question concerns whether distinct continuous histories can merge under generic perturbations of a delayed linear evolution. It requests qualitative recovery of histories, without a stability bound for an inverse.

## Known results and scope

All bounded linear history operators are allowed, including distributed delays. The quantifiers cover every real initial time and every later finite time on the entire continuous history space; injectivity does not require surjectivity or a bounded inverse.

Hale and Oliva proved density in the full coefficient space and explicitly left residuality unresolved. Their residual result for a restricted step-measure class and their local short-time residual result do not establish the displayed statement. Hale and Verduyn Lunel repeat the full-space residuality question immediately after Theorem 5.2 on printed page 380.

# 546. Uniqueness of the closed genus-one self-shrinker in three dimensions

**Area:** Geometric analysis and mean curvature flow
**Status:** 🔵 OPEN
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3`$ be a smooth, compact, connected, embedded surface without boundary, diffeomorphic to $`S^1\times S^1`$. For its position map $`X`$, suppose
```math
\Delta_\Sigma X=-\tfrac12X^\perp,
```

where $`\Delta_\Sigma=\mathrm{div}_\Sigma\nabla_\Sigma`$ and $`X^\perp`$ is the normal projection of the position vector. Must $`\Sigma`$ be an orthogonal image of the classical Angenent torus, normalized to satisfy this equation?

No rotational symmetry is assumed for $`\Sigma`$. The extinction center and scale are fixed by the displayed equation. The question excludes immersed or noncompact competitors.

## Application

Self-shrinkers describe self-similar singularity models for mean curvature flow. A positive answer would classify the compact embedded toroidal models in three dimensions. It would also identify the rotational doughnuts obtained through different existence constructions, whose equality should not be assumed in advance.

## Status review

The September 2026 reference explicitly leaves uniqueness open. Its profile-curve results concern rotationally symmetric surfaces and impose additional conditions; they do not imply the unrestricted classification above. The later genus-one constructions reviewed here must not be treated as closed examples without establishing compactness.

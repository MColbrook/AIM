# 894. Dimension-free bounded approximation for Euclidean Lipschitz-free spaces

**Area:** Functional analysis and metric geometry
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

For a pointed subset $`M\subseteq(\mathbb R^n,\|\cdot\|_2)`$ containing $`0`$, let $`\mathrm{Lip}_0(M)`$ be the real-valued Lipschitz functions vanishing at $`0`$, with the Lipschitz norm, and define
```math
\mathcal F(M)=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{\delta_x:x\in M\}
\subseteq\mathrm{Lip}_0(M)^*,\qquad \delta_x(f)=f(x).
```


**Question.** Is there a universal finite constant $`\Lambda\ge1`$, independent of both $`n`$ and $`M`$, such that every such $`\mathcal F(M)`$ has the $`\Lambda`$-bounded approximation property?

Here this property means that, for every norm-compact $`K\subseteq\mathcal F(M)`$ and $`\varepsilon>0`$, there exists a finite-rank bounded linear operator $`T:\mathcal F(M)\to\mathcal F(M)`$ with
```math
\|T\|\le\Lambda,\qquad \sup_{u\in K}\|Tu-u\|<\varepsilon.
```

The question asks for one finite bound, and does not require the metric approximation property $`\Lambda=1`$.

## Application

A universal approximation bound would control finite-rank approximation of transportation-cost spaces generated by Euclidean data without a dimension-dependent operator-norm loss.

## Known results and scope

The eligible source records a $`C\sqrt n`$ bound for all Euclidean subsets, following Lancien and Pernecká. The eligible paper itself also proves a dimension-independent bound for ambient nets with a fixed separation/covering ratio: an (a,b)-net is a-separated and b-dense in the whole ambient Banach space, and Theorems 2.6 and 1.5 give a K-Schauder basis with K depending only on b/a. Arbitrary Euclidean subsets need not be ambient nets, and the question seeks a constant independent of M as well as n. Pernecká and Smith prove the metric approximation property for compact subsets with suitable almost retractions from neighborhoods, including compact convex subsets. Talimdjioski's 2024 thesis explicitly retains the dimension-independent question. Later work on snowflaked metrics and property (V*) has different conclusions or dimension-dependent constants.

# 423. Compact resolvent for kinetic Fokker–Planck and Witten operators

**Area:** Spectral theory and mathematical physics
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`n\geq1`$ and $`V\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb R)`$. On the unweighted space $`L^2(\mathbb R^n_x\times\mathbb R^n_v)`$ set $`\Phi_V(x,v)=|v|^2/2+V(x)`$ and initially define on $`C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^{2n})`$

```math
K_V=v\cdot\nabla_x-\nabla V(x)\cdot\nabla_v-\Delta_v+\frac{|v|^2}{4}-\frac n2,
\qquad
W_V=-\Delta_{x,v}+\frac{|\nabla_{x,v}\Phi_V|^2}{4}-\frac{\Delta_{x,v}\Phi_V}{2}.
```


Use the maximally accretive closure of $`K_V`$ and the nonnegative self-adjoint closure of $`W_V`$. Is

```math
(I+W_V)^{-1}\text{ compact}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(I+K_V)^{-1}\text{ compact}
```


true for every such $`n,V`$? No growth or integrability assumption is imposed on $`V`$. This is the remaining direction of Helffer–Nier Conjecture 1.2 [1, p. 3]; their Theorem 1.1 proves the converse direction for general smooth potentials.

## Application

The kinetic operator couples spatial transport, the force generated by $`V`$, and diffusion in velocity. The implication would turn a self-adjoint confinement test into a compactness criterion for its non-self-adjoint kinetic counterpart, supporting the analysis of discrete spectra in kinetic models. The statement does not assert a quantitative rate of return to equilibrium.

# 1057. The limiting contact geometry of corotating vortex-patch pairs

**Area:** Nonlinear waves, fluids and kinetic equations
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

For the two-dimensional incompressible Euler equation, consider the García–Haziot global branch $`\mathscr C`$ of symmetric, corotating pairs of equal-vorticity patches $`D_2=-D_1`$ that bifurcates from a pair of point vortices. As the branch parameter tends to infinity, **must its limiting configuration consist of the two patches touching at a point where their boundaries develop a $`90^\circ`$ corner**? This is the Overman conjecture for this two-patch branch. The statement concerns the fate of this branch; it does not merely ask whether some cornered rotating patch exists.

The global bifurcation theorem proves that at least one degeneration occurs: vanishing of a relevant relative fluid-velocity quantity or vanishing distance between the patch boundaries (the Oberwolfach report also lists a polar-graph alternative). It does not prove that contact occurs, that the corner forms there, or that these degenerations coincide. The conjecture excludes the other possibilities as sole limiting scenarios.

## Application

This would identify the large-amplitude endpoint geometry of a physically important two-vortex equilibrium family, turning numerical contact-and-corner evidence into a theorem about the global Euler branch.

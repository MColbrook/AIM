# 783. Irrational ratios of hyperbolic three-manifold volumes

**Area:** Hyperbolic geometry and low-dimensional topology
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`M`$ and $`N`$ be complete orientable hyperbolic three-manifolds of finite volume, with sectional curvature normalized to $`-1`$. Their volumes are positive real numbers.

**Question (Thurston).** Can $`M`$ and $`N`$ be chosen so that $`\mathrm{Vol}(M)/\mathrm{Vol}(N)\notin\mathbb{Q}`$?

The ratio question is one precise interpretation of Thurston's phrase “not all rationally related.” A stronger interpretation asks whether the set of all such volumes spans an infinite-dimensional vector space over $`\mathbb{Q}`$; that stronger question is separate and is not asserted here.

## Application

An irrational ratio would show that the hyperbolic volume spectrum contains at least two distinct rational directions. This would advance our understanding of the arithmetic information encoded by three-manifold geometry and would rule out the possibility that every manifold volume is a rational multiple of one fixed volume.

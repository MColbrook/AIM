# 701. Smooth Levi-flat hypersurfaces in the complex projective plane

**Area:** Complex geometry and foliation theory
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Prove or disprove that there is **no nonempty compact, boundaryless, $`C^\infty`$ embedded real hypersurface** $`M\subset\mathbb{CP}^2`$ that is Levi-flat.

Here $`M`$ has real dimension three. If $`J`$ is the complex structure of $`\mathbb{CP}^2`$, Levi-flatness means that the rank-two real distribution

```math
H_pM=T_pM\cap J(T_pM),\qquad p\in M,
```

is involutive. Equivalently, $`M`$ is foliated by immersed complex curves, or its Levi form vanishes identically. One may take $`M`$ connected, by passing to a connected component.

No real-analyticity, symmetry, prescribed topology, compact-leaf or global defining-function assumption is imposed. Singular hypersurfaces and pieces with boundary are outside this question.

## Application

The question links dynamics of complex foliations to global complex geometry. A hypothetical hypersurface would divide the projective plane into two Stein domains; ruling it out would give a basic obstruction to such smooth Levi-flat boundaries.

# 503. The plane fixed-point problem for nonseparating continua

**Area:** Geometry and topology
**Status:** 🔵 OPEN
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`K\subset\mathbb R^2`$ be a nonempty compact connected set such that $`\mathbb R^2\setminus K`$ is connected. Must **every continuous self-map** $`f:K\to K`$ have a fixed point?

Such a $`K`$ is a *nonseparating plane continuum*. It can be represented as an intersection of a nested sequence of planar topological disks. The question concerns all continuous self-maps, without an extension or orientation assumption.

## Application

A positive answer would extend the familiar fixed-point property of a disk to arbitrary nonseparating plane continua. The challenge is the possible wild local structure: local connectivity or even arcwise connectivity gives positive results, while nonplanar tree-like continua without the property do not settle the plane case.

# 765. Does a contractible open four-manifold without a Gabai splitting exist?

**Area:** Topology of open manifolds
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Does there exist a Hausdorff second-countable topological $`4`$-manifold $`M`$, noncompact and without boundary, which is contractible but admits no open sets $`U,V\subseteq M`$ satisfying
```math
M=U\cup V,\qquad U\cong\mathbb R^4,\quad V\cong\mathbb R^4,
\quad U\cap V\cong\mathbb R^4?
```

Such a cover is called a **Gabai splitting** or the **double $`4`$-space property**. All equivalences above are homeomorphisms. No smoothness, compact-core or compactification hypothesis is imposed.

## Application

This asks how the topology of a contractible open four-manifold constrains its decomposition into Euclidean patches, linking end topology with embeddings and gluing.

A non-splittable dunce-hat spine does not by itself produce a counterexample: the source warns that even a four-ball can have such a spine. Exotic smooth structures on $`\mathbb R^4`$ are also insufficient, because the cover is topological.

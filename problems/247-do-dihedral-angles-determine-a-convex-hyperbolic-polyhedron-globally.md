# 247. Do dihedral angles determine a convex hyperbolic polyhedron globally?

**Area:** Hyperbolic geometry and rigidity
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`P`$ and $`Q`$ be compact convex polyhedra in hyperbolic three-space $`\mathbb H^3`$ with the same combinatorial type. Suppose every edge of $`P`$ has the same dihedral angle as the corresponding edge of $`Q`$. **Must $`P`$ and $`Q`$ be congruent by a hyperbolic isometry?** A pair of noncongruent polyhedra satisfying these conditions would settle the question negatively.

## Application

Dihedral angles provide natural coordinates for constructing hyperbolic polyhedra and for describing their doubles as cone-manifolds. A global rigidity theorem would show when these local geometric data identify the entire polyhedron.

## Known results and scope

Montcouquiol [1, Theorem 2 and discussion after Theorem 3] proves *local* rigidity: nearby compact convex hyperbolic polyhedra of fixed combinatorial type are locally parameterized by their dihedral angles. The paper explicitly leaves global congruence open. Andreev's theorem covers the nonobtuse-angle case [1]. Belletti [2] proves that a separate volume conjecture would imply global hyperbolic Stoker rigidity, but this is conditional. Sun [3, Section 6.2] explicitly records the hyperbolic version as open in 2025. Wang and Xie [4] address the distinct Euclidean Stoker conjecture; spherical global rigidity is false [1, 3].

# 283. Invariant point processes for a branching random walk

**Area:** Branching particle systems and extreme-value probability
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`\mathcal P`$ be the space of point measures $`D`$ on $`\mathbb R`$ such that $`D((x,\infty))<\infty`$ for every real $`x`$. Atoms are counted with multiplicity, and the empty measure is allowed. Equip this space with the sigma-field generated by the tail counts $`D\mapsto D((x,\infty))`$, for real $`x`$. Write $`\tau_aD`$ for translation by $`a`$, and put
```math
\mathcal P_*=\{D\in\mathcal P:\max\mathrm{supp}D=0\}.
```

Fix a random offspring measure $`Z=\sum_j\delta_{z_j}\in\mathcal P`$ and $`\alpha>0`$. Assume
```math
\mathbb E[Z(\mathbb R)]>1,\qquad
\mathbb E W=1,\qquad
W=\sum_j e^{\alpha z_j},
```
and the nonlattice condition
```math
\mathbb P\{\mathrm{supp}Z\subset a\mathbb Z+b\}<1
\quad\text{for every }a>0,\ b\in\mathbb R.
```
All sums run over actual atoms. Define
```math
\mu=\mathbb E\sum_j z_je^{\alpha z_j},
\qquad
V=\sum_j(z_j)_+e^{\alpha z_j},
\qquad
\log_+u=\max(\log u,0),
```
with $`\log_+0=0`$. Require either the boundary assumptions
```math
\mu=0,\qquad
\mathbb E\sum_jz_j^2e^{\alpha z_j}<\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E\!\left[W(\log_+W)^2\right]
+\mathbb E[V\log_+V]<\infty,
```
or
```math
0<\mu<\infty,\qquad \mathbb E[W\log_+W]<\infty.
```

Call a random $`E=\sum_i\delta_{e_i}\in\mathcal P`$ invariant if, for independent copies $`Z^{(i)}`$ of $`Z`$, independent of $`E`$,
```math
\sum_i\tau_{e_i}Z^{(i)}
\quad\text{is }\mathcal P\text{-valued and has the same law as }E.
```
Does there exist a single probability law $`\mathcal D_*`$ on $`\mathcal P_*`$, depending only on the fixed offspring law and $`\alpha`$, such that these invariant laws are exactly the laws
```math
E\ \stackrel d=\ \sum_i\tau_{\alpha^{-1}\log S+\xi_i}D_i?
```
Here $`(\xi_i)`$ is a Poisson point process with intensity $`e^{-\alpha x}\,dx`$, the $`D_i`$ are independent with law $`\mathcal D_*`$, and $`S`$ is any finite nonnegative random variable, independent of both. The Poisson process, decoration sequence and $`S`$ are mutually independent. When $`S=0`$, the sum means the empty measure.

This is Maillard and Mallein's Conjecture 1.3. Its equation is $`\mathcal E=\mathcal E\circledast\mathcal Z`$. The same normalized decoration law must describe every invariant distribution. No finite-intensity assumption is imposed on $`E`$.

## Application

The equation describes a spatial particle configuration whose distribution survives a generation of independent reproduction and displacement. The proposed classification would identify its possible cluster structure. Decorated Poisson processes also describe the extreme particles of several branching models; a classification supplies structural information about a limiting process once its existence is established.

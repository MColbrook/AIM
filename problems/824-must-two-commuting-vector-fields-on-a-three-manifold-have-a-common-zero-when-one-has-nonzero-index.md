# 824. Must two commuting vector fields on a three-manifold have a common zero when one has nonzero index?

**Area:** Smooth dynamics and differential topology
**Status:** 🟡 PARTIAL
**Last checked:** 2026-10-30

## Problem statement

Let $`M`$ be a smooth three-manifold, and let $`X,Y`$ be commuting $`C^1`$ vector fields on $`M`$. Let $`U\subset M`$ be a relatively compact region such that $`X`$ has no zero on $`\partial U`$, so its Poincaré–Hopf index $`\mathrm{Ind}(X,U)`$ is defined. If $`\mathrm{Ind}(X,U)\ne0`$, must $`X`$ and $`Y`$ have a common zero in $`U`$?

Equivalently, if $`Y`$ has no zero in $`U`$, must $`\mathrm{Ind}(X,U)=0`$? This is [1, Conjecture 1.2], with no geometric restriction on the set where $`X`$ and $`Y`$ are collinear.

## Application

The index detects zeros of one vector field. The conjecture asks whether that topological obstruction also forces a fixed point for the joint local dynamics generated by two commuting flows in dimension three. It would extend the known real-analytic result to $`C^1`$ fields without conditions on their collinearity set.

## Known results and scope

Bonatti and Santiago prove the $`C^1`$ assertion when the collinearity set of $`X`$ and $`Y`$ in $`U`$ lies in a compact boundaryless $`C^1`$ surface [1, Theorem A]. This is an added hypothesis, not part of Conjecture 1.2. Alvarez, Bonatti and Santiago establish further $`C^3`$ cases: relevant periodic orbits of $`Y`$ are partially hyperbolic, or the flow of $`Y`$ preserves a transverse plane field [2]. A later note by Bonatti still describes the analogous $`C^2`$ common-zero assertion as a conjecture [3]. None of these results supplies the unrestricted $`C^1`$ case stated above.
